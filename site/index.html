A naive reading of the raw feed jumps when VIIRS joins the record, because the instrument changed, not the burning. If harmonisation worked, that step should close. It is measured, not asserted, and it recomputes live for whatever area you select.

05

How the record was made

  1. One schema

    Both sensors collapse to a single table; MODIS 0–100 confidence maps onto the VIIRS low/nominal/high scale.

  2. Grid, not pixels

    Detections bin to a 0.25° grid and monthly windows. Raw point counts are never compared across sensors.

  3. Cell-days

    The unit is one cell, one day, any burning. Nearly invariant under pixel size and platform count.

  4. A shared floor

    Both sensors clip to the weakest fire MODIS reliably reports, from its own FRP distribution.

  5. Calibrated on the overlap

    Median VIIRS-to-MODIS ratios per cell and calendar month, fitted on the years both flew.

  6. Then tested

    The series is plotted across the join and the residual step is measured. Section 04 is that test.

Treat two satellites like two log sources: normalise to one schema, baseline the record, alert on deviation. The same discipline that runs a security operations centre, pointed at wildfire.

  • Detections are not burned area. One huge fire and five small ones can count the same.
  • Cloud and overpass gaps hide fires — a quiet pixel is not a certified absence of fire.
  • Industry burns too. Gas flares and volcanoes give some cells a permanent false fire season.
  • Weak factors are labelled. Every scaled value carries its calibration sample size.
